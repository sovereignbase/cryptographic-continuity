This document defines the Verifiable Continuity Attestation Scheme and its verification method.

The attestation target is the information carried in the claim field. The scheme itself does not assign semantics to the claim.

The attester is the asserting Actor. Depending on the claim, that Actor may be the subject, holder, issuer, or another role described by the claim. Those roles belong to the claim semantics, not to the attestation scheme.

The attestation proves that the asserting Actor made the claim, assuming continued sole control of the private keys in the continuity chain.

The scheme combines claim attestation with Actor continuity. Actor continuity is implemented as verifiable continuity of protected signing keys.

Verification is based on information already trusted by the verifying Actor. A verifying Actor may verify another Actor if it holds a trusted key that can validate the other Actor’s continuity chain.

A trusted key is a key stored in the verifying Actor’s own state by its controller for the purpose of verifying another Actor.

The controller is always a human. The controller may act under a delegated task from an organization, but the controller is still the human who can access a device capable of loading and operating the Actor’s state.

If the verifying Actor cannot verify another Actor from its trusted information, it must report to its controller that the other Actor could not be verified or is unknown.

Recognition uses the same attestation mechanism. A trusted Actor may issue a verifiable recognition credential about an otherwise unknown Actor. The verifying Actor can verify that credential through the trusted Actor’s continuity and present the recognized information to its controller.
